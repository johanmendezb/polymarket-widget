Each candidate gives you: question, category, resolution date, 24h volume and
quoted spread. You are deliberately NOT given prices. Do not guess at them and
do not reason about which side looks cheap.

RANK the candidates by where an independent outside view is most likely to be
informative. That is a question about:

- Tractability. Is this the kind of question evidence can actually settle?
- Evidence availability. Is there recent, dated, primary-source reporting?
- Time horizon. Near-term questions are more tractable than distant ones, and a
  model's information advantage decays quickly.
- Resolution clarity. Ambiguous criteria make any estimate unreliable regardless
  of how good the reasoning is.

It is NOT a question about where the biggest edge is. You cannot know that, you
do not have the prices, and pretending otherwise is the failure mode this
ranking exists to avoid.

an outside view, say so and rank it last rather than inventing a rationale.
